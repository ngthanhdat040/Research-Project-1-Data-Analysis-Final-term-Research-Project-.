# Research-Project-1-Data-Analysis-Final-term-Research-Project
Implementation of Fundamental Data Analysis Method: Descriptive Statistics &amp; Exploratory Data Analysis and Basic Predictive as well

# PROJECT ABSTRACT
This project focuses on evaluating the operational status of an e-commerce platform using an empirical dataset. Findings from the Olist platform (Brazil) reveal 
a critical risk governed by the Pareto Principle: approximately 80% of total revenue is generated by only 20% of active sellers. This extreme revenue concentration
poses high instability and vulnerability to systemic disruptions for the marketplace. Driven by this urgency, the research aims to identify the key driver variables 
of high-performing sellers, thereby formulating data-driven strategies to bridge the performance gap for the bottom 80% underperforming sellers left behind.

To address this core problem, the study applies Quantitative Research methodology on a large-scale historical transaction dataset comprising over 100,000 real-world
records. The analytical pipeline combines Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) and interactive dashboarding in Tableau with rigorous econometric techniques in Stata, 
including Pearson correlation analysis, statistical hypothesis testing (T-test, ANOVA), and multiple/simple linear regression models.
Contrary to conventional assumptions, the analysis reveals that service quality metrics (review scores) and logistics efficiency (shipping cost and delivery duration) are not the root causes of the revenue disparity on Olist. Instead, the primary driver enabling top-performing sellers to dominate the market lies in a "Dual-Diversification" product portfolio strategy. This strategy expands horizontally (selling across multiple product categories to broaden customer acquisition and optimize cross-selling effects) while simultaneously penetrating vertically (offering dozens of unique SKUs within each active category).

# RESEARCH CONTEXT 
Global E-Commerce Shift & The Brazilian Market: Since the early 2000s, the global economy has shifted rapidly toward e-commerce. Brazil quickly emerged as the largest e-commerce market in Latin America. However, severe market fragmentation across multiple major platforms (e.g., Mercado Livre, Americanas, Amazon Brazil) alongside high logistics costs posed significant growth barriers for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs).
The Rise of Olist: Founded in 2015 by Tiago Dalvi, Olist addressed these structural hurdles by offering an all-in-one sales enablement ecosystem. By consolidating operations through a single contract, Olist allows SMEs to list products under the highly trusted Olist Store, granting them immediate access to massive traffic and optimized logistics networks without individual marketing overheads.
Core Problem Statement (Pareto Imbalance): Despite Olist's ecosystem advantages, preliminary analysis of historical dataset reveals a severe revenue concentration strictly following the Pareto Principle (80/20 Rule): ~80% of total platform revenue is generated by a top 20% minority of high-performing sellers. This structural vulnerability poses a high churn risk and threatens platform sustainability. Consequently, a comprehensive investigation into seller performance drivers is required to formulate strategic interventions that bridge the performance gap between top- and low-performing sellers.

# DATA ANALYSIS WORKFLOW 
The analytical process adheres to a structured, data-driven problem-solving framework, tailored to maintain methodological rigor, consistency, and logical coherence throughout the research. The analytical pipeline is executed through the following 6-stage workflow:
## 1. Data Ingestion & Loading: Extracting and aggregating raw multi-table relational datasets into the analysis environment.
## 2. Data Preprocessing & Cleaning: Handling missing values, filtering anomalies/outliers, transforming variable formats, and preparing clean relational structures.
## 3. Exploratory Data Analysis (Initial EDA): Profiling data distributions, summary statistics, and preliminary structural patterns across seller segments.
## 4. Problem Framing & Research Questions: Formulating key analytical objectives, defining hypothesis frameworks, and targeting specific revenue-driver variables.
## 5.Advanced EDA, Visualization & Hypothesis Testing: Combining interactive Tableau dashboards with Stata statistical tests (T-test, ANOVA, Regression) to evaluate empirical relationships.
## 6. Insight Interpretation & Strategic Recommendations: Synthesizing findings to derive data-driven business conclusions and actionable platform-level interventions.

# DATA STRUCTURE OVERVIEW
The analysis utilizes the Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset by Olist, a structured tabular dataset capturing multi-dimensional e-commerce operations in Brazil.
Dataset Scale & Coverage:
- Volume: 109,341 records, where each row represents a unique transaction/order aggregated across multiple Brazilian retailers.
- Timeframe: Historical order records spanning from March 10, 2016, to January 20, 2017.
- Attributes: 28 variables covering customer profiles, seller identifiers, product details (pricing, category), order financials (total transaction value, freight cost), and customer satisfaction metrics (review scores and feedback comments).
Spatial Data Integration:
- Includes a supplementary Geolocation Dataset mapping Brazilian postal codes (ZIP codes) to precise geographical coordinates (latitude and longitude) for spatial and logistics analysis.

# PROJECT OUTPUT 
Comprehensive analysis of Olist’s transaction data reveals severe structural revenue inequality governed by the Pareto Principle (80/20 Rule): a top 20% minority of sellers controls 81.49% of total platform revenue. To eliminate single-point dependency and foster long-term ecosystem resilience, this project delivers a three-pillar, data-driven platform intervention strategy:
## 1. Portfolio Diversification Strategy (The Primary Revenue Lever)
Data proves SKU volume and category breadth account for nearly 30% of revenue variance between seller tiers (Top sellers average 36 SKUs across multiple categories vs. Low sellers averaging 5 SKUs in 1–2 categories).
- Horizontal Expansion: Encourage low-performing sellers to expand across at least 3–4 distinct product categories to maximize customer touchpoints, optimize cross-selling effects, and mitigate seasonal demand fluctuations.
- Vertical Penetration: Diversify SKUs within active categories (variations in color, size, and price points) to improve search algorithm indexing and conversion rates.
- Data-Driven Trend Signals: Leverage platform-wide transaction data to generate periodic market trend reports, empowering sellers to stock inventory based on empirical demand rather than intuition.

## 2. Platform Policy Architecture & Friction Reduction
To lower operational barriers associated with portfolio expansion for small and medium-sized sellers:
- Incentivized Listing Policies: Introduce temporary listing fee waivers or commission discounts (e.g., first 60–90 days) for sellers entering new, unfamiliar categories—similar to Amazon's New Selection Incentives.
- Targeted Logistics Subsidies: Apply selective shipping fee subsidies for heavy/bulky new SKUs during initial expansion phases to eliminate cart abandonment friction caused by freight costs.
- Seller Intelligence Dashboards: Provide seller-facing analytics dashboards featuring top-growth categories, competitive price benchmarks, and category-level conversion metrics to eliminate information asymmetry.

## 3. Strategic Price Repositioning & Operational Support
An empirical counter-intuitive insight reveals that top-performing sellers do not compete on low prices—they maintain ~40% higher average price points (149 vs. 106 currency units) while driving vastly higher order volumes, leveraging price as a quality signal.
- Value-Based Pricing Guidance: Educate low-tier sellers against destructive price wars ("race to the bottom"), shifting focus toward premium product listings, professional store branding, and richer descriptions to transition into higher price brackets.
- Operational Automation for Top Sellers: Protect the platform’s revenue core by equipping high-volume sellers with smart inventory management, delivery tracking, and automated customer service tools to prevent review score degradation caused by fulfillment bottlenecks.
